About

Megan Wild, and a low camera

I am a nature and wildlife photographer. I work from Staten Island, New York, and from Puerto Rico.

Ground level

I work from as low as the camera will go. It means getting down onto the sand or the mud, waiting far longer than seems reasonable, and letting the animal decide how close it wants to come. Shot from there, an animal stops being a sighting and becomes a portrait — you are at its height, looking at it rather than down at it.

Light is the subject of these pictures, not the lighting for them. Most frames are decided before anything walks into them: the light crosses the water a particular way for a short while each day, and the work is being there when it does.

Where the work comes from

Staten Island is home. Harbour, marsh, beach and woodland sit within a few miles of each other, so a morning can hold several kinds of picture without going far.

Puerto Rico is the other half of the work — mangroves, dry forest, coast and the smaller weather that moves across an island. Two places, one habit of looking closely.

Who this is for

First, people who watch: nature lovers and wildlife watchers, the sort who keep binoculars by the door and want to see what a place actually looks like up close.

Second, editors, contest judges and picture buyers. Licensing, publication and print enquiries are all welcome — use the contact line below.
